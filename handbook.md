# Backstreets Police Department – Complete SOP Changes & Updated Rules

This combines both documents:

1. The original LSPD SOP change announcements.
2. The current Backstreets Police Department SOP document.

I've consolidated the information into one master reference, including the additional rules in the full SOP, later updates, and conflicting rules that need clarification.

The goal is to have everything in one place so officers can review the current policies without searching through multiple Discord announcements.

# 1. General Department Policies

## Chain of Command

- Officers must respect the chain of command.
- Follow instructions and corrections from higher-ranking personnel.
- Operational questions should go to your supervisor first.
- If your supervisor is unavailable, escalate through the chain of command.
- Failure to follow the chain of command may result in disciplinary action.

## Activity Requirements

- Minimum activity: 5 hours per week.
- Inactivity exceeding one week without an approved LOA results in one strike point per week.
- LOAs must be submitted through PD Emails.
- LOAs can last up to one month before renewal is required.

## Off-Duty Regulations

- Officers must be fully clocked out.
- Police equipment must be stored in personal lockers.
- Police uniforms and department vehicles cannot be used off duty.
- Officers cannot intervene in crimes while off duty.
- Officers must call law enforcement and comply with responding officers.
- Unprofessional or aggressive off-duty behavior can result in disciplinary action.

## Badges

- Officers must carry their badges while on duty.
- When requested, officers must provide their name, callsign and proof of identity.
- Failure to identify yourself may result in disciplinary action, demotion or termination.

# 4. Search, Seizure and Probable Cause

## Reasonable Suspicion

- Reasonable suspicion is a suspicion without concrete evidence.
- Officers may temporarily detain a suspect for investigation and public safety.
- Searching is not permitted under reasonable suspicion alone.
- The detention must be explained as an investigation, not an arrest.
- Maximum detention: 24 in-head minutes.

## Probable Cause

- Evidence connecting a suspect to a crime.
- Examples include matching shell casings, witnessing a crime or discovering relevant contraband during an investigation.
- Probable cause permits an arrest.

## Section 4.2.2 – Search and Seizure

- Searches require probable cause or explicit consent.
- Officers may search or frisk suspects in the field when proper probable cause or consent exists.
- Contraband may be seized in the field or at HQ.

## Shots Fired Investigations

- GSR testing does not require consent for 10-13 calls.
- Negative GSR results mean the suspect should not be searched and is free to leave.
- Positive results require Miranda Rights and detention.
- Officers may look for bullet casings.
- A discovered casing permits a frisk for a weapon.
- Weapon serial numbers should be crossmatched against recovered casings.
- Officers must consider whether the weapon was used in self-defense.

## Drug Sale Investigations

- Non-K9 officers need bodycam footage of a confirmed handoff before engaging.
- Officers should communicate with the suspect and investigate before proceeding.
- Exceptions exist for immediate threats or ongoing criminal activity.
- After a confirmed handoff, the suspect may be arrested.
- Officers should not immediately draw weapons.
- After cuffing, suspects should receive medical attention if needed before searches.
- Connected vehicles may also be searched.
- Illegal drugs and items may be confiscated.
- K9 deployment requires clear and reasonable suspicion.
- K9s cannot be used on every routine traffic stop without justification.

# 5. Scene Conduct and Miranda Rights

## Scene Completion

- When the majority of PD is wiped and EMS has arrived, the scene is over.
- All evidence from that scene becomes invalid.

## Miranda Rights – Section 4.4

- Officers must read Miranda Rights to suspects in custody when detained or arrested and being questioned.
- Failure to read rights does not invalidate an arrest.
- Statements made before rights are read are inadmissible evidence.

## Evidence and Reporting

- Officers must log all appropriate reports.
- Gun confiscations, mugshots and evidence must be recorded.
- Proper documentation is required through the department's designated channels.

# 6. Use of Force – Section 5

## General Use-of-Force Levels

| Level   | Type                    |
| ------- | ----------------------- |
| Level 1 | Officer presence        |
| Level 2 | Verbal communication    |
| Level 3 | Hand-to-hand / tackling |
| Level 4 | Non-lethal force        |
| Level 5 | Deadly force            |

General principles:

- Preserve human life whenever possible.
- Use objectively reasonable force.
- Match force to the danger presented.
- Lethal force requires an imminent threat, intent and a weapon or other identified threat.
- Officers must communicate before escalating whenever possible.

## Matching Force – Section 5.1

- Officers must match their force to the situation.
- A weapon being brandished requires at least one verbal command to drop it.
- A weapon pointed at officers or civilians creates an immediate public safety concern.
- If an officer goes down or activates a panic button, responding officers may use matching force.

## Taser – Section 5.2

- Officers must give verbal warnings before deployment.
- Officers must announce when they are going for a taser.
- A 15-second interval is required between taser shots.
- Do not repeatedly tase a suspect.
- Do not tase suspects on ladders, rooftop edges or near dangerous drops.
- Avoid tasing people standing in water.
- Anyone tased in water must immediately be removed from the drowning risk.
- A suspect attempting to enter a vehicle may be tased before entering.
- A suspect already inside a vehicle may be tased after announcing deployment.

## Tackling – Section 5.3

- Officers must verbalize the tackle warning.
- Officers must announce that they are going for a tackle.
- Do not repeatedly tackle someone without giving them a reasonable opportunity to comply.
- Allow a tackled suspect to stand and surrender.
- A second tackle may be used if the suspect gets up and remains non-compliant.

## Cuffing – Section 5.4

- Instruct suspects to put their hands up and face away.
- Officers must announce when they are going for cuffs.
- Non-compliance may require escalation.
- Suspects who are tackled or tased may be cuffed while on the ground.
- Do not spam cuffs if the first cuff breaks.
- Cuffing someone while running or walking is considered power cuffing.
- Power cuffing can result in a strike and possible government punishment.
- Hard cuffs are a last resort and should only be used when a suspect is not properly roleplaying.
- Regular hard-cuff abuse results in disciplinary action.

## Non-Lethal Shotgun – Section 5.5

- Cannot be used as a primary taser.
- 10-second cooldown between shots.
- Command+ authorization required.
- Class 3 certification required.
- Verbal commands must be given before use.
- Abuse can result in privilege revocation and disciplinary action.

Approved situations:

- Bank heists.
- House/store robberies.
- MLO breaches and gang blocks.
- Executing signed warrants.
- Crowd control.
- Prison transports.
- Hostage rescues.
- Certain vehicle extractions involving non-compliant occupants.

## Police Shields – Section 5.6

- Only the black shield labeled Police Shield is permitted.
- Maximum of 3 shields per scene.
- Class 1 weapons only while equipped.

Approved situations:

- Bank heists.
- House/store robberies.
- MLO breaches and gang blocks.
- Executing signed warrants.
- Crowd control.
- Prison transports.
- Hostage rescues.

## Air-1 – Section 5.7

- A co-pilot is mandatory.
- Passengers may respond with matching force if the aircraft is shot at.
- Class 1 weapons are restricted to specified vehicle-disabling or water-dumping situations during an active 10-80.
- Refer to the Air-1 SOP for additional requirements.

# 8. Arrests and Processing – Section 7

## General Processing

- The primary officer should transport arrested suspects.
- A secondary officer may assist when multiple suspects need transportation.
- Other officers must return to patrol after the scene is Code 4 unless handling evidence.
- Officers must not remain at the station unnecessarily.
- Additional units may escort processing officers if there is a suspected breakout attempt.

## Maximum Penalties

Maximum fine

# $200,000

Maximum sentence

# 200 months

## Processing Procedure

- Read Miranda Rights once the suspect is cuffed.
- Ask whether the suspect wants an available lawyer.
- If requested, the suspect contacts DOJ services once uncuffed in the cells.
- Requesting a lawyer does not automatically reduce jail time.
- Ask whether the suspect requires medical attention.
- Contact EMS when necessary.
- If the suspect is fully down, CPR is required until EMS arrives.
- Conduct a final search in the station garage.
- Obtain a clear mugshot and fingerprint.
- Masks or obstructing accessories may be removed for identification.
- Failure to provide fingerprints results in maximum fine and sentence.
- Complete the MDT report and charges.

## Holding Cells

- Maximum holding time: 24 in-head minutes.
- Exception: A suspect requesting a lawyer may be held until their lawyer arrives.

## Insufficient Funds

- Officers may add one month per $1,000 owed when the suspect cannot afford their fine.
- This cannot exceed the 200-month maximum.

## Guilty Pleas

- Officers may reduce the sentence by 50% when a suspect pleads guilty.
- The reduction is at the arresting officer's discretion.

# 9. Gun Confiscation and Buybacks

## Section 7.2 – Confiscation

Every confiscated firearm requires:

- An MDT report, even if no arrest is made.
- A Dyno log in `#weapon-confiscation`.
- A weapon strike check when applicable.
- Placement in the appropriate Criminal CID evidence stash.
- If a suspect escapes or pops their head, the firearm must be placed in the CID Evidence stash.

## Confiscation Rules

| Circumstance                 | Required action                       |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Gun not used + valid license | Return before jailing                 |
| Gun not used + no license    | $50,000 charge; return before jailing |

Weapon strikes are tied to individual serial numbers.

| Strike | Consequence            |
| ------ | ---------------------- |

## Section 8.2 – Weapon Buybacks

- Only F9-registered weapons qualify.
- Non-F9 weapons cannot be bought back.
- Weapons with 3 or more violent strikes are ineligible.
- A weapon license is not required.
- All buybacks must be recorded using Dyno in `#gun-buyback`.
- Failure to log buybacks may be treated as PD corruption.

| Weapon class | Buyback price |

## Weapon Licenses – Section 8.1

- Licenses are only issued to individuals with clean records.

- Licenses may be revoked when weapons are used in violent felonies.

# 10. Warrants, Search Warrants, Bail and Expungements

## Section 9.1 – Warrants

Official warrants

- Issued by Detectives/FIB through the MDT.
- Valid for one week.
- Dropped if the suspect cannot be found after one week.
- Officers may attempt service at a player's location once every two hours.
- If the suspect is not present, officers must leave.

Unofficial in-city warrants

- Corporal+ may issue them over the radio.
- Require proper bodycam and evidence.
- Last 3 hours in head.
- Cannot be entered into the MDT.
- Must be communicated verbally through radio.
- The current SOP limits these warrants to criminal charges and excludes attempted murder of a government official.

Additional restrictions

- Officers cannot lure or entrap suspects to serve warrants.
- Warrants must be served naturally.
- Warrants cannot be served inside Green Zones.

## Section 9.2 – Search Warrants

- Detectives may request search warrants through DOJ Discord.
- Proper evidence is required.
- Search warrants may be used to collect crime evidence or make an arrest inside a property.
- A signed warrant is required before breaching a player's house.
- Unauthorized breaches can result in SOP failure or PD corruption.

## Section 9.3 – Bail

- Bail is generally available for non-violent crimes.
- Officers have discretion over whether to accept bail.
- Violent-crime bail requires High Command approval.
- Suspects must plead guilty.
- A suspect who accepts bail cannot later challenge the charges through DOJ.
- Suspects must be informed of this before accepting.
- Bail costs $1,000 per month of the original sentence.
- Bail is added on top of the original fine.

Example:

| Item              | Amount       |
| ----------------- | ------------ |
| Original fine     | $50,000      |
| 50-month sentence | $50,000 bail |
| Total             | $100,000     |

## Section 9.4 – Expungements

- At least 24 hours must pass before expungement.
- Each charge costs $10,000.
- Officers must remove the charge from the incident.
- Save the incident.
- Add a note to the individual's profile.

The change announcements initially transferred expungements to DOJ, but a later update returned responsibility to PD until DOJ is fully operational.

# 14. Traffic Stops – Section 13

- Traffic stops are for road violations and other appropriate reasons.
- Activate lights and sirens to signal the driver.
- Once stopped, turn off sirens but keep lights flashing.
- Officers must formally introduce themselves.
- Request the driver's license, registration and insurance.
- Check the driver's information and outstanding warrants.
- Check whether the vehicle is stolen.
- Officers may issue a warning or ticket when appropriate.
- Tickets must be roleplayed and entered using the correct fine command.
- Additional units may be requested when a vehicle or occupant presents a reasonable concern.
- Only one additional unit should respond to a routine traffic-stop backup request.

# 15. Code 5 Felony Stops – Section 14

- A Code 5 stop requires probable cause.
- Additional units should be requested.
- Officers must coordinate through radio.
- The primary officer directs the stop.
- Officers must ensure that all occupants are accounted for.
- The vehicle is checked after occupants are secured.

## MDT Vehicle BOLOs – Section 14.2

- Vehicle BOLOs last a maximum of 30 minutes.
- Proper RP interaction is required before issuing one.
- Valid reasons include:
  - Losing sight of a vehicle during a 10-80.
  - Vehicle involvement in shots fired.
  - Vehicle involvement in a bank robbery.
  - Confirmed drug sales.
- BOLOs must be deleted or checked after 30 minutes.

## Section 15.4 – PIT Policy

- Senior Officer+ or the highest-ranking active officer must confirm the status.

- The timer may be requested 30 seconds after Code Yellow.
- A 30-second cooldown applies between PIT attempts.
- The highest-ranking officer on duty must authorize the timer.

# 21. Hostage Situations

## Primary Objective

- Preservation of life is the highest priority.
- Hostage safety takes precedence over immediate suspect apprehension.
- Officers must allow realistic and fair roleplay.

## Initial Response

- Establish a perimeter.
- Assign a primary negotiator.
- Maintain controlled radio communications.
- Allow the situation to develop naturally.

## Negotiations

- Negotiations must be conducted in good faith.
- Suspect demands must be taken seriously.
- Officers cannot intentionally stall negotiations for tactical advantages.
- Communication must remain professional and in character.

## Hostage Demands

- One demand per hostage.
- Maximum of three demands per scene.
- Physical item exchanges are prohibited.
- Acceptable demands include safe passage and reasonable time allowances.
- Unrealistic demands should be negotiated down rather than immediately denied.

## Prisoner Transport and Life-for-Life

- Sergeant+ officers have discretion to approve or deny prisoner transports as hostage demands.
- Decisions must consider officer headcount and city call volume.
- Prison transports and Life-for-Life exceptions are restricted to Gang Captain+.

## Use of Force During Hostage Situations

- Force is a last resort.
- Lethal force requires lethal intent.
- Tactical escalation must be justified by the RP.
- The highest-ranking officer on scene must approve major tactical escalation.
- Officers should not rush negotiations.
- Force should not be initiated while a weapon is actively aimed at a hostage unless reasonable options have been exhausted.

# 24. LSPD and FIB Jurisdiction – Section 21.3

The current SOP includes a specific jurisdictional policy:

Independent Governance

- LSPD is an independent governing body.
- FIB has no supervisory authority over LSPD personnel or departmental operations.

Joint Investigations

- LSPD assistance to FIB during an investigation is considered a joint operation.
- LSPD officers take orders exclusively from the highest-ranking LSPD officer involved or present.
- FIB agents may request assistance and coordination.
- LSPD should assist when possible.
- If an LSPD operation or warrant results in an incident involving an injured or attacked FIB agent, the underlying case remains under LSPD jurisdiction.

Conduct

- Mutual respect between departments is mandatory.
- Insubordination, disrespect or overstepping jurisdictional boundaries must be documented and reported to LSPD High Command.

# Final Summary – What Officers Should Know

These are the most important policies to remember:

1. Minimum activity is 5 hours weekly.
2. Three active strikes disqualify officers from divisions.
3. Bodycams must be active and footage must be retained.
4. Searches require probable cause or explicit consent.
5. Miranda Rights must be read according to custody and questioning requirements.
6. Hard cuffs are a last resort.
7. Hard cuffs, power cuffing and equipment abuse can result in strikes.
8. All confiscated weapons require MDT reports and Dyno logs.
9. Only eligible F9 weapons can be bought back.
10. Search warrants must be signed before house breaches.
11. Warrants cannot be served inside Green Zones.
12. Standard T-count is 8 officers, subject to listed exceptions.
13. Gang-block calls require at least 5 officers on duty.
14. Gang vs. gang scenes have special procedures.
15. Code Orange has been removed.
16. Code Yellow is required for PIT authorization.
17. Hostage negotiations must be conducted in good faith.
18. SWAT requires 3 operators for casual activation and 4 for full activation.
19. LSPD maintains its own chain of command separate from FIB.
20. The maximum fine is $200,000 and the maximum sentence is generally 200 months, subject to the sentencing discrepancy noted above.
